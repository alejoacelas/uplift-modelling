A THEORY NOTE / INTERACTIVE

What does the
functional form buy us?

A model of AI adoption should explain persistent differences between people and lasting gains from coaching. The question is which restrictions on behavior produce those patterns—and which additional predictions come along with them.

THE OBJECT

y(t) = E[Σj vjaj(t)] / Σj vj

Expected value captured as a share of available value. Each application is adopted or not: aj ∈ {0, 1}. We study the expected path, not a simulated individual.

THE ENGINE

ẏ = h(t, y) · (p − y)

p is the share of value reachable under the person’s current approach. h is the adoption hazard among remaining reachable applications. The equation is exact for a common exogenous hazard; with heterogeneous hazards, h is their value-weighted average among the remaining applications.

02 / LOCATE THE INTERVENTION

A level shift is not a mechanism.

Start from limited reach. Coaching could change the reachable set, adoption speed, or current adoption. These interventions have different implications even if all three raise observed value initially.

03 / TAKE THE MODEL TO THE FACTS

What would actually rule something out?

Select a candidate stylized fact. The table distinguishes compatibility from an explanation. These are restrictions of the displayed models, not claims that the observations have been established.

Finite data do not establish a ceiling.

Very low positive hazards can look like inaccessible applications. To distinguish them, observe the remaining opportunities and longer follow-up—not just a flat aggregate curve.

A curve need not identify its mechanism.

Feedback in the current state and a hazard that rises with elapsed time can trace the same path. An intervention that moves adoption at the same elapsed time helps distinguish them.

Targeting needs a response function.

In the reach model, the long-run gain is Δp. A low starting p gives more room, but does not tell us Δp. Who benefits most remains unidentified until coaching responsiveness is measured or assumed.

The invariant is often more revealing.

Under a common constant hazard, normalized gaps decay at the same proportional rate across people. Different plateaus with parallel log-gap paths support a shared adjustment process.

Working model, not an empirical result.
